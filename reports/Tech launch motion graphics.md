# Where to browse real tech launch motion

Three kinds of site cover this: dedicated launch-video libraries, motion-design platforms and studio portfolios, and code-based video frameworks with their showcases. For real tech launch films, start with the **eChai Video Library** (about 1,740 startup videos, including 2024–2026 Linear, Notion, Stripe, Vercel and Raycast entries) ([eChai](https://echai.ventures/videos?category=launch)), **Scrollsnacks** (500+ launch videos split into 4,000 tagged hook/problem/CTA sections, updated every weekday) ([HuntScreens](https://huntscreens.com/products/scrollsnacks)) and **Flowjam's free launch-video library** ([Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/launch-videos)). For high-end brand launch films (Apple, Google, OpenAI, Figma), the best sources are studio portfolios (Buck, Ordinary Folk, Studio Dumbar, basement.studio) and the editorial archives of **Stash Media** and **Motionographer**. For motion graphics made with code, **Remotion's showcase** is the only framework gallery that names "launch videos" as a category ([Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev/showcase/)), and 2026 brought a wave of agent-written launch videos (Remotion Agent Skills, HeyGen HyperFrames, Anthropic's Claude Motion). Verification is limited. The research network blocked **echai.ventures, flowjam.com and remotion.dev**, and nearly every other host could not be fetched directly. Most entries below were confirmed only through search results and third-party listings, so counts and freshness are approximate. Each table has a status column, and anything marked unverified should be checked in a browser before you rely on it.

**How to read the status column.** *Fetched live*: the page was loaded or responded during research (mostly GitHub pages, plus Dribbble). *Search-confirmed*: the URL appeared as a live search result, but the page itself was not loaded. *Blocked, snippet only*: the host was refused by the research network, so details come from search snippets or mirrors. *Unverified*: a well-known URL from general knowledge, or a URL built from a site's pattern; neither fetched nor found in search.

## Eight dedicated libraries curate real launch videos, and three are current

Only a handful of sites exist purely to collect real product launch videos, and **most are run by video agencies as lead magnets**. Their curation leans toward the SaaS/YC explainer-launch style rather than Apple-tier brand films. eChai ranks videos by how many founders recommend them and lets you filter by purpose (launch, explainer, keynote, pitch, ad, "AI-made"), industry and region. Its index has individual pages for films such as "Introducing Linear Releases" (2026) ([eChai](https://echai.ventures/videos/linear-introducing-linear-releases)), the Vercel Ship 2024 keynote ([eChai](https://echai.ventures/videos/vercel-vercel-ship-2024-keynote)), Stripe Sessions 2025 ([eChai](https://echai.ventures/videos/stripe-stripe-sessions-2025-product-keynote)) and Make with Notion 2025 ([eChai](https://echai.ventures/videos/notion-make-with-notion-2025-product-launch-keynote)), plus many YC launch videos such as Palmier (YC S24) ([eChai](https://echai.ventures/videos/palmier-palmier-yc-s24-launch-video)). Scrollsnacks is the most analytical. Each video is broken into timed sections that describe spoken lines, on-screen text and visual effects, a "Stitch" feature combines sections into storyboard reels, and an **MCP server lets AI assistants query the library** ([ProductCool](https://www.productcool.com/product/scrollsnacks)).

Agency blog roundups fill out the long tail. They work as one-off swipe files, not maintained libraries. **Vidico's free swipe file of 100+ motion graphics videos** is filterable by industry ([Vidico](https://vidico.com/news/motion-graphics-examples/)). Advids publishes many 30-example lists, including one on motion-graphics launch videos ([Advids](https://advids.co/blog/30-top-motion-graphics-launch-video-examples-to-ignite-curiosity)) and one on SaaS launch videos ([Advids](https://advids.co/blog/saas-product-launch-videos)). Smaller lists come from Moonb (12 launch videos sorted by format) ([Moonb](https://www.moonb.io/blog/product-launch-video)), B2W (33 animated product videos) ([B2W](https://www.b2w.tv/blog/best-animated-product-video-examples)) and VidPros ([VidPros](https://vidpros.com/product-launch-video/)). The a16z Speedrun essay on making a viral launch video adds the strategy behind the form ([Substack](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-viral-launch-video?open=false)). One adjacent showcase is **Motion.so's "Made with Motion"**, a vendor gallery of launch films and demos shown with their prompts ([Motion.so](https://motion.so/made-with-motion)). All of these were search-confirmed only.

The research found **no** GitHub "awesome-launch-videos" list, no confirmed Are.na channel and no maintained X list. The closest GitHub hits were about conference talks and launch communities, not videos ([ecosyste.ms](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2FsoGeneri%2Fawesome-launch)). Mobbin, Godly, Land-book and Lapa Ninja had no dedicated launch-video filter in search results. That is a gap in the evidence, not a confirmed absence.

### Official company archives are keynote-centric

Apple keeps the deepest official archive. Its **Apple Events video podcast has replays of most events since 2007** ([Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/id275834665)). Stripe hosts its Sessions 2026 keynotes on session pages ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/at/sessions/2026/opening-remarks-and-product)), and Vercel does the same for Ship ([Vercel Ship 2025](https://vercel.com/ship/2025), [closing keynote](https://vercel.com/ship/session/closing-keynote)). Short feature-launch films from Linear, Raycast, Figma, Notion and OpenAI seem to live mainly on their YouTube/X accounts and changelogs. Search could not confirm official archives for any of them, which is why aggregators like eChai matter.

No major design platform verifiably has an official "tech product launch" motion category. You find high-end launch films through studio portfolios plus a few motion-editorial sites. **Stash Media's per-studio tag pages** are the best editorial archive for this. On Dribbble, the `product_launch` tag and the launch-video search page both responded during research. They returned bot challenges, which shows the pages exist, and user collections such as "Promo Video" are live ([Dribbble](https://dribbble.com/benhaynes/collections/5342109-Promo-Video)).

## Confirmed code-made tech videos are rarer than the hype

Public, primary-source proof that a well-known company's launch film was made in code is scarce. **No source confirmed that any official Vercel, Linear, Raycast or Supabase launch video was coded**, and the Supabase films that exist are explicitly unofficial. The documented cases are framework demos and smaller companies that wrote up their process. **GitHub Unwrapped** is the flagship: a yearly personalized year-in-review video that the creator describes as "essentially a demo project" for Remotion, built by the Remotion team (not GitHub) on Next.js or Vite plus AWS Lambda ([github-unwrapped-2023](https://github.com/remotion-dev/github-unwrapped-2023)). **Fireship's "This video was made with code. But how?"** was itself rendered in Remotion, with code by William Candillon ([source](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fwcandillon%2Fremotion-fireship)). Dardo studio's 28.8-second **Superame commercial** carries "Made with Remotion 4.0.520" in its file metadata ([Dardo](https://dardo.studio/en/blog/remotion-video/)).

## Conclusion

The landscape splits by budget tier. SaaS and YC launch videos are well catalogued by agency-run libraries such as eChai, Scrollsnacks and Flowjam. Apple-, Google- and OpenAI-tier launch films are not collected anywhere central; they sit scattered across studio portfolios, and you find them through Stash and Motionographer. The practical way to browse is therefore two-pronged: use eChai or Scrollsnacks for structure and volume, then use studio case pages for craft. For code-made motion, the gap between hype and evidence is the main finding. Code is clearly how many new launch videos get made in 2026, especially agent-written ones, but almost no big-brand launch film has public proof that it was coded. Treat "made in Remotion" claims about famous companies as unverified until a primary source says so.

The biggest verification debt is the three blocked hosts: eChai, Flowjam and Remotion's showcase. These are also the three most important sources in this report. Loading them in a normal browser is the first thing to do, both to confirm current counts and to see which, if any, recognizable tech brands appear in Remotion's showcase.
